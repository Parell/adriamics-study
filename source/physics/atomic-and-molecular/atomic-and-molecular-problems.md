For a hydrogen atom, what is the energy of the $n=2$ level in the Bohr model?

:::solution
Use $E_n=-13.6\ \text{eV}/n^2$ for hydrogen ($Z=1$):

$$
E_2=-\frac{13.6}{2^2}\ \text{eV}=-3.40\ \text{eV}.
$$
:::

-->

An atom falls from $E_i=-3.40\ \text{eV}$ to $E_f=-13.6\ \text{eV}$. Find the emitted photon energy.

:::solution
The photon carries the decrease in atomic energy:

$$
E_\gamma=E_i-E_f=10.2\ \text{eV}.
$$
:::

Is an electric-dipole transition with $\Delta\ell=0$ allowed?

:::solution
No. The electric-dipole rule is $\Delta\ell=\pm1$, so $\Delta\ell=0$ is forbidden in this approximation.
:::

What is the ground-state energy of $\mathrm{He}^+$ in the hydrogen-like model?

:::solution
Use $E_n=-13.6Z^2/n^2\ \text{eV}$ with $Z=2$ and $n=1$:

$$
E_1=-13.6(2)^2\ \text{eV}=-54.4\ \text{eV}.
$$
:::

Can the hydrogen-like energy formula be used exactly for neutral helium? Explain.

:::solution
No. Neutral helium has two electrons, so electron–electron repulsion and shielding alter the energy levels. The formula is exact only for a one-electron ion in the ideal Coulomb model.
:::
